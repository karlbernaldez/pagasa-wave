# Wave Forecasting Concepts

## Purpose
This document explains marine and wave forecasting concepts used by WaveLab chatbot responses.

## Common Questions

### Question: What is wave forecasting?
Answer:
Wave forecasting is the process of estimating future marine wave conditions based on forecast inputs, environmental conditions, and operational analysis.

### Question: Is WaveLab the simulation engine?
Answer:
No. WaveLab is the operational platform for managing forecast products and workflows. External forecasting systems may provide forecast data inputs.

## Core Terms

### Wave Height
Definition:
Wave height describes the vertical size of wave conditions represented in forecast products.

### Significant Wave Height
Definition:
Significant wave height is a standard marine metric representing average wave conditions based on the highest portion of observed or modeled waves.

Chatbot note:
Users may refer to this as SWH or significant height.

### Wave Direction
Definition:
Wave direction describes the represented movement or source direction of wave energy.

### Wave Period
Definition:
Wave period is the time interval between successive wave crests.

### Peak Period
Definition:
Peak period refers to the dominant wave energy period.

### Swell
Definition:
Swell refers to wave energy that has propagated away from its generating weather source.

### Wind Sea
Definition:
Wind sea refers to locally generated wave conditions caused by nearby wind forcing.

### Forecast Horizon
Definition:
Forecast horizon is how far into the future a forecast product extends.

### Forecast Product
Definition:
A prepared operational marine forecast output.

## Interpretation Guidance

Question: What does higher wave height mean?
Answer:
Higher wave heights generally indicate rougher marine conditions.

Question: What does longer wave period suggest?
Answer:
Longer wave periods are often associated with swell-dominated conditions.

## Chatbot Rules
The chatbot should explain these terms using WaveLab terminology and avoid inventing unsupported operational thresholds.
